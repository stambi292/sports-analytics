Bundesliga 2026/27 · summer transfer spending vs points after 9 games

Did the money buy points?

Bundesliga clubs spent €782m on new players this summer. Hoffenheim's €18.5m record signing has played 30 league minutes. Nine games in, I put every club's spending next to its points to see who got value, and who is paying the most for each point.

One signing, a big share of the budget

Hoffenheim paid a club-record €18.5m for Nathan de Cat. That was 28% of everything they spent this summer. He has played 30 Bundesliga minutes, 169 minutes in all, and is reported to be out for months. Hoffenheim sit 9th on 12 points. When so much of a budget sits in one player, one injury can wipe out most of the summer's investment.

What clubs can learn

Selling is not a death sentence

Leipzig sold €176m and are 4th. Freiburg made €45m net and are 3rd. Net spend and points barely move together (correlation 0.11). Clubs can sell their stars at the right price if they reinvest well.

Big fees need big minutes

Leverkusen spent the most (€157m) and pay €9.8m per point, twice Bayern's rate. A fee only pays back when the player plays: track availability and fit before paying a premium.

Spread the risk

Werder are 6th on €7.3m across 16 signings. Several affordable players spread injury and form risk better than one record fee, as Hoffenheim's 28% bet shows.

Nine games is early: points will move, and spending here covers transfer fees only, not wages. The ranking will be worth checking again at the winter break.
