Germany · golden era vs today

Where did Germany's talent go?

From 2006 to 2014 Germany reached three World Cup semi-finals and won the 2014 World Cup with a young squad built at home. Since then: two group-stage exits and a last-32 defeat to Paraguay. Ten years of player data show where the pipeline broke, and what would fix it.

By Stephen tAMBI · World Cups 1990–2026 · player ratings 2014/15–2023/24

The pipeline broke after 2018

Young players aged 21 or under in Europe's top five leagues with a potential rating of 83 or more: the future stars. Germany peaked at 26 in 2017/18, the year after the Confederations Cup and U21 Euro wins, then fell to 7. Switch countries to compare.

Ratings from the FIFA / EA FC games (FIFA 15–22, FC 24); 2022/23 is missing. Game ratings are not truth, but they are judged the same way every year, which makes trends comparable.

Fewer world-class players than in 2018

Players rated 80 or more in the top five leagues. Germany fell from 46 to 33. Spain has about twice as many every year.

The missing positions: strikers and full-backs

Players rated 80+ by position, Germany against Spain, averaged over all nine seasons. Germany produces midfielders and attacking players like Musiala and Wirtz. It barely produces full-backs and strikers. That is why a 33-year-old Füllkrug is still the answer up front.

Average number of players per season, 2014/15–2023/24. Position = first listed position in the game.

Young Germans play less in their own league

Two measures of opportunity: the share of first-choice starters who are local, and how many local players aged 21 or under start for each club. In 2014/15 a Bundesliga club started almost one young German on average; since 2019 it has been closer to one in every two or three clubs. France gives its youngsters about one starting place per club every season.

What Spain does differently

Spain did not win by having more teenagers on the pitch: its clubs start young locals no more often than Bundesliga clubs. The difference is depth and trust in its own players. On average 58% of LaLiga starters are Spanish, against 43% Germans in the Bundesliga, and Spain produces world-class players in every position, including about 10 full-backs a season against Germany's 0 to 4. Spain then won Euro 2024 and the 2026 World Cup.

Six fixes, each with a number to track

Data cannot coach a player, but it can show where to invest and whether it works. Each fix comes with a target taken from Germany's own best years, so progress can be measured every season.

Method and sources

  • World Cups 1990–2022: squads, birth dates, results and goals from the Fjelstul World Cup Database (github.com/jfjelstul/worldcup). 2026: results (7–1 Curaçao, 2–1 Ivory Coast, 1–2 Ecuador, 1–1 Paraguay, lost on penalties) and squad ages from match reports and squad lists.
  • Player ratings: FIFA 15–22 (sofifa data, via github.com/eddwebster/football_analytics) and EA FC 24 squads (github.com/fpessolano/FantasyFootball). Top divisions of England, Spain, Germany, Italy and France only. FIFA 23 (2022/23) was not available.
  • Definitions: "future star" = aged 21 or under with potential 83+; "world-class" = overall 80+; nationality as listed in the game.
  • Limits: game ratings are a proxy, not scouting reports, and the starter flag is not real minutes. Real minutes by nationality and age (from the league's own data) would make every chart here sharper.
