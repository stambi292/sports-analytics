Kick-off for whom?

Late-night and Monday games are one of the oldest complaints of Spanish football fans: Alavés supporters once unfurled a giant TV with the words “switch off the TV, save football”. I mapped every LALIGA kick-off since 2015/16 to see how the schedule has changed, which clubs carry the awkward slots, and what it asks of away fans.

Independent analysis by Stephen tAMBI on public fixture data. Not affiliated with LALIGA or any club.

What LALIGA could change

Late and weeknight slots exist for good reasons: they spread games across TV windows, keep the big matches in prime time for Spanish and international audiences, and pay for the clubs' broadcasting income. The ideas below keep those slots and change who gets them.

How it was built

  • Data: Kick-off times are local Spanish time.
  • Definitions: "late" = 20:45 or later. Monday and Friday games count only in regular weekend rounds, not in midweek rounds where the whole round is played Tuesday to Thursday.
  • Distance: straight-line distance between the two clubs' stadium cities, from approximate coordinates.
  • Not covered: attendance and TV audiences are not in the public data used here, so the page shows the schedule, not its effect on crowds. Matching attendance by slot would be the natural next step.
