UEFA Nations League · money, games and who benefits · 2010–2025

Follow the moneyDid UEFA create the Nations League to make money?

The Nations League replaced most friendlies in 2018. UEFA's own financial reports show what happened to the money, and every international result since 2010 shows what happened to the games.

Stephen tAMBI · data to 2024/25

The money almost doubled

UEFA sells TV and sponsorship rights for all European national-team games as one package, in cycles. Per season, the package was worth almost twice as much once the Nations League was in it.

€ million per season: each cycle's total divided by its seasons (2014–18: €1,033m over 4; 2018–22: €1,937m over 4; 2022–28: about €3bn over 6, forecast). The package covers Euro and World Cup qualifiers, the Nations League and some friendlies, so not all of the rise is the Nations League.

But most of it goes to the countries

Yes, the countries make money too. UEFA passes almost all national-team income on to its 55 member associations. What it keeps covers running the competitions.

What a country earns from the Nations League

Every team gets a fee just for playing, set by its league. Winning the group pays the fee again. The four finalists share €16.5m more plus ticket money.

€ million. The per-league fees are worked out from UEFA's totals: these four amounts reproduce the starting-fee and group-winner totals of all three editions exactly. Separate from this, each association also receives a share of the qualifier and media income above.

Method and sources

  • Money: UEFA financial reports 2018/19, 2020/21, 2022/23 and 2024/25. Revenue and distributions are as recognised in each season's accounts, not cash paid. 2021/22 revenue is derived from the 2022/23 report ("€104m higher than 2021/22").
  • Games: every international result 2010–2025 from the open international results dataset (martj42 on GitHub). UEFA teams = the 55 that have played in the Nations League. Qualifiers = Euro and World Cup qualifying.
  • Not measured: TV audiences and attendances, which UEFA does not publish game by game, and players' club workloads.
