The injury bill

This season's headlines are full of players who cannot play: Hoffenheim's record signing out for months, Arkadiusz Milik with 34 minutes since 2024. Behind every one is a wage bill. I looked at what injuries cost each Bundesliga club last season, and what cutting the time players spend out would be worth.

Average cost per injury

The Bundesliga had more injuries than any other top league over five seasons, but each one cost clubs the least, under €0.1m, because Bundesliga wages are lower and absences shorter. In the Premier League each injury costs almost three times as much.

The bill by season

After two seasons near €149m, the bill fell by €38m in 2024/25.

What would fewer days out be worth?

The wage cost of injuries is a club's injured players' daily wages times the days they miss. So every day of absence a club prevents saves money. Pick a club and set how much it cuts its days out.

What clubs can do

€3.4m

Shorten the absences

Hoffenheim's injured players were out 31 days on average, the league's longest; the league average is 19. Getting back to average would have saved about €3.4m, a fifth of what Hoffenheim paid for Nathan de Cat. Faster diagnosis and return-to-play plans matter as much as preventing the injury.

€16k

Protect the expensive players

Each day an injured Bayern player missed cost about €16,000 in wages, almost six times Werder's rate. Load management, rotation and individual training plans pay back fastest where wages are highest.

U21

Manage young players' load

Howden found under-21s were, for their numbers, the most often injured group in the top leagues over four seasons. Clubs buying young talent should plan their minutes and recovery, not just their fee.
