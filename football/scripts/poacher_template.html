Goals against expected goals, season by season

Expected goals (xG) measures the quality of each chance: how often an average player would score it. A striker who scores more than his xG is finishing better than average.

Method and sources

  • Season goals, xG, shots and minutes: Understat, through the public striker-explorer snapshot on GitHub. Understat covers the top five leagues only, so 2014–17 (Nürnberg, 2. Bundesliga Hannover) and 2021/22 (Werder in 2. Bundesliga) are missing.
  • 2026/27: 9 goals in 656 minutes over 8 games (VAVEL, ~10 Nov 2026). No public xG yet, so the regression estimate uses his career chance rate.
  • Shot map: StatsBomb open data, free for non-commercial use with credit to StatsBomb. 20 shots, 10 games, about 470 minutes.
  • Career goals per game: Wikipedia, league games only.
  • Calculator: goals in a game are treated as a Poisson count, so the chance of at least one goal is 1 − e−λ, where λ = xG per 90 × minutes ÷ 90 × finishing ratio (goals ÷ xG).
