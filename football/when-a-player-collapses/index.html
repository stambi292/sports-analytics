Sudden death in football · what the data says

When a player collapses

Every year, players collapse on football pitches. Most of them are amateurs, and many could be saved. Data from FIFA's Sudden Death Registry shows that the first few minutes, and whether a defibrillator is nearby, decide who survives.

Independent analysis by Stephen tAMBI, based on the FIFA Sudden Death Registry and news reports.

Why players collapse

Causes were confirmed by autopsy or medical records in 211 of the 617 cases (34%).

All confirmed cases

Heart causes, players aged 35 and under

Over 35, coronary artery disease caused 76% of cardiac cases. Under 35, causes differed by region: cardiomyopathy led in South America (42%), coronary artery anomalies in North America (33%) and sudden unexplained death in Europe (26%). Among young players, 9% of deaths were commotio cordis: a blow to the chest, usually from the ball, that stops the heart. Jesús de Miguel's death in November 2026 followed a blow to the chest in a collision.

What clubs and players can do

  1. Put a defibrillator (AED) at every pitch, for training as well as matches, and make sure everyone knows where it is.
  2. Train players and coaches in CPR. In the registry, players were most often the first to help, before the ambulance arrived.
  3. Act at once: call the emergency number, start chest compressions, and use the AED as soon as it arrives.
  4. Report every case to the FIFA Sudden Death Registry, so the data keeps improving.
